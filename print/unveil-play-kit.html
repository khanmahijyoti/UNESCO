Unveil — play kit

Print at 100% scale, margins minimum, background graphics on (the dealer card uses a dark label). Pages:

  • 1–2 — Vote slips, 4 per page. One per player per play. Print 4 pages for a 5-player / 2-play workshop; more for larger tables.
  • 3 — Player rules cards, 4 per page. One per player. Contains no build table.
  • 4 — Dealer / facilitator card. Print one, and keep it off the table.
  • 5 — Facilitator score sheet. One per session.

Unveil — building the deck

Build one extra · deal N · burn one unseen
  1. Draw the build for your player count, blind, from each of the three labelled stacks.
  2. Shuffle the build together.
  3. Deal one sleeved pair to each player.
  4. Burn the last card face-down without looking at it. Set it aside untouched.
  5. Randomise who starts. Do not use seat order twice running.

The burn is the randomiser. It exists so that you do not know the exact rogue count either — you know the pool, not the deal. Never look at the burned card until cleanup.

At cleanup

  • Every dealt card retires for the rest of the session — press pairs and audience cards alike. A redealt headline is a dead card: its truth is already public.
  • File the burned card yourself, privately. Do not turn it face-up in front of the table — that confirms the exact composition of the play just finished.
  • Re-pair slips to clue cards using the answer key (headline → code). Slips carry no code.

How many plays you have

The Rogue stack (10 cards) runs out first at nearly every table size — it is the binding constraint, not the Real stack. Roughly: 4–6 players ≈ 6 plays · 7 players ≈ 4 · 8–9 players ≈ 3 (audience stack binds) · 10 players ≈ 3. At 7+ players the box does not hold a 1,000-point evening; cap the target at 800.

Debrief — two minutes after each reveal

  • Which clue type persuaded you — Precedent, Context, or Defense? Fabrications lean hardest on Precedent: "this sort of thing happens" is not evidence that this happened.
  • Did anyone publish a fake because it confirmed something they already believed?
  • Did anyone reject a real story purely because it sounded absurd? Ask this one every single time. It is the question players resist, and the one the Real database exists to force.
